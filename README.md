# Multi-Wavelength Emission from Dark Matter Annihilation

Well motivated extensions of the Standard Model (SM) of Particle Phyiscs predict dark matter annihlation into SM particles. If dark matter has a mass of order 10-100 GeV and annihlates to unstable SM particles, these particles will rapidly decay, ultimately leading to a shower of stable particles such as photons, electrons (and positrons), and others. These photons would be expected to have gamma ray energies and a production rate proportional to the square of the dark matter density. Interestingly, there is evidence of an excess of gamma rays coming from the centers of the Milky Way and Andromeda (M31) with a spatial profile that is consistent with what would be expected from dark matter annihlation. These gamma rays can be fit by 10-50 GeV dark matter annihlating to bottom quarks. Although the gamma ray excess is consistent with having come from dark matter annihlation, there are other competing explanations that the community is split between.

If indeed the gamma rays are coming from dark matter annihilation, there would be a population of high energy electrons and positrons from these annihilation events. These electrons and positrons would propogate around the galaxy and emit radiation in various wavelengths. In this project, we calculate the rate of electron and positron injection from dark matter annihlation into bottom quarks in M31. We then model the propogation of these electrons and positrons through the galaxy using the diffusion-loss equation with the most relevant diffusion and energy loss mechanisms for M31. We solve the equation numerically to find the distribution of electrons and postrons today. Using teh distriubtion of electrons and positrons, we calculate the radio emission produced from electrons and positrons accelerating in galactic magnetic fields. Using the prediction of radio emission from the previous steps and 8.35 GHz radio data from M31, we set constraints on dark matter annihlation.
